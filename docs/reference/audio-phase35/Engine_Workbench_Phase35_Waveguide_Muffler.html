Simulation scope, physical assumptions and A/B interpretation

The HTML workbench is the development reference. All models are independently selectable to compare simplified and reduced-order physical behavior. Quick acoustic settings only initialize visible sliders once. Existing V3 backfire and four-wheel tire sound generators are unchanged.

Phase 3 mechanical output: Simulated integrates per-cylinder 720° cycles with bore/stroke, rod ratio, clearance volume, compression, crank-angle Wiebe heat release, fuel mass and mixture quality. Cylinder pressure produces indicated pressure-volume work; speed-dependent mechanical friction and accessories are subtracted, and net torque changes crankshaft RPM using rotational inertia. The resulting signed torque drives wheel force and affects the existing engine pressure-pulse sound. Mechanical Approximate preserves the original drive and audio equations for comparison.

Important limitations: Cylinder pressures, heat-release constants, compression ratio defaults, flywheel inertia, friction curves and acoustic scaling are illustrative. Energy timing is tracked on fixed crank-angle substeps; the chamber model is not a full closed thermodynamic energy-balance solver. Crankshaft RPM remains coupled to a simplified transmission/clutch solver. Turbo rotor, intake manifold pressure and thermal boundary states are still reduced-order previews, not fully coupled gas dynamics. A low-speed idle stabilization term stands in for actual idle-bypass control. Pressure, crank torque and efficiency readings must be calibrated against independent measured data before being used as physical targets.

Independent fidelity: Fuel maps, cylinder charging/valves, cylinder combustion control, mechanical crank output, drivetrain, turbo, tires and exhaust chemistry can each be toggled without enabling the others. Mechanical Simulation necessarily uses the selected fuel/charge stream to determine torque even when the legacy Fuel → drivetrain shortcut is unchecked. An approximate fuel or combustion setting is a deliberately approximate input to the simulated crank, and should be interpreted accordingly.

Testing: Use the repeatable full sequence for scenario coverage; compare matched WAV renders under mechanical Approximate and Simulated. Monitor cylinder pressure versus crank angle, pressure-volume cycles, indicated/net torque, RPM, fuel energy, air/fuel quantities, boost, clutch slip and individual tire slip. Export graph CSV for independent analysis; keep test seed, fuel maps, gear ratios and fidelity switches identical between runs.
