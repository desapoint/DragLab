## Physical bench graphs and exports

The standalone DrivingTest scene and garage entry use the race solver without finish/reward limits. Recording stays off by default. With recording enabled, every 120 Hz sample is retained, including staging, shifts, lift, neutral coast, braking and failure; full and continued recordings replay the same captured hardware and controls. The acoustic bench remains the separate static-RPM/sound laboratory.

Select a graph family, then its channel. Families expose output/pedals/gears/boost, all four wheel speeds and measured slips, normal loads and contact forces, actual wheel drive/brake/reaction torque, body/wheel/flywheel energy, accumulated drive/contact/differential/brake/resistance work, coupling losses, mass/fuel, oil/coolant and loss rates, tire life/heat/sliding work, cylinder contributions, and every installed component's health/temperature. Part health/heat families are paged in groups of twelve. Absent component values are -1 (health plots display -100%); absent cylinder contributions are zero. These are explicit absence markers, not physical negative health.

Wheel torque and force channels average measured impulses across the full tick. Brake torque is actual applied angular impulse, clipped at zero wheel momentum, rather than requested pedal capacity. Contact reaction torque equals contact force times tire radius. Drive torque includes constraint projection/differential transfer. Calculated body/wheel energies use current mass/inertia/speed; air/rolling resistance channels expose the model's current force parameters. Work is accumulated history, not a claim that all drivetrain losses are separately conserved. Contact loss includes differential constraint loss, so do not sum those channels as independent losses. Mechanical heat remains the existing simplified model. Graphs and CSV are observations and introduce no second physics implementation.

Speed, shaft torque and boost/oil-pressure graphs follow saved display units; CSV headings retain explicit canonical units. Every selectable measured channel is included in CSV as well as the previous export fields. The selected channel header shows its live value even with recording off. Previous runs remain layered chronologically. Graph/config clicks preserve pedal ownership.

## Project authoring

Open **DragLab → Vehicle Workbench** and select a Vehicle Definition. Its Inspector remains the full engine/vehicle authoring surface. The integrated section selects a compatible part family and definition, shows meaningful stat effects and part-owned tuning permissions, and expands all editable authored parameters.

Edits are drafts until **Apply project part override**. **Discard draft** restores the applied value; **Restore authored default** removes that definition's override. Applied data lives in `Game/Assets/DragLab/Resources/Data/part-overrides.json` and is used by catalog resolution in races, inspection, shop and physical bench. No overrides are supplied by default. Restart Play/player after authoring to recreate cached garage catalogs; current captures/in-progress runs retain their original definitions. Identity, category, fitment, bore and rank remain stable; changed identities require a separately authored definition/migration. Validation rejects unsupported schema, duplicates, unknown IDs, incompatible identities, nonfinite/negative quantities, invalid enums/flags/ranges and unusable wear/thermal parameters before an atomic file replacement.

These are developer project parameters, separate from player purchase/install and tune permissions. Fixed parts stay fixed in player tuning. Captured fitted part definitions remain immutable when later project data changes. Legacy rank controls below the integrated section remain balance probes, and the contact lab remains an isolated wheel-solver experiment; current all-part integration checks resolve actual hardware.

The mixed-part test assembly lets you select or remove individual compatible definitions across all slots, reset to the OEM recipe, inspect dependency errors/warnings, benchmark the resolved build and export its repeatable full physical JSON/CSV scenario. It uses the tune controls below through the actual resolver and does not alter player assets.

Workbench buttons run integrated tooling/calibration, the catalog/configuration matrix, or owned-condition recovery tests and export their reports. Terminal equivalents: `tools/Invoke-Unity.ps1 -Action IntegratedVerify`, `-Action SliceTwoAudit`, and `-Action RecoveryVerify`. `-Action BuildWindows` runs the entire current foundation, integrated tooling/matrix and scene gates before building. `tools/Test-SliceTwoIntegration.ps1` adds the final isolated Windows recovery, full gameplay, driving/failure, inventory/reset, keyboard/race, durability, audio-level, inspection and summary workflows under a fresh run name. The full catalog/foundation gate can take tens of minutes; use targeted verification actions for iteration and reserve the complete gate for a checkpoint. Use `-SkipBuild` only after building the current source; `-ShowPlayer` makes the rendering/input review visible.

## Calibration policy

`reports/phases/2l/calibration.csv` records four-car OEM ET/trap/F–S ratings, actual braking from 35 m/s and manual/sequential comparisons on matching OEM builds. Manual-shift comparison drivers use the same automatic launch and state-based legal shift timing to isolate shifting; they are scripted controls, not human driving measurements. `controlled-runs.csv` compares stock, 250 kg payload, 5 L fuel, 1.3× tire diameter, 18 psi, cold starts, reduced wear/fuel, and no wear/fuel. Corresponding JSON captures reproduce the controls/builds. `*-integrated.csv` exports full measured timelines. The catalog-wide matrix additionally covers every compatible part, transmissions/induction/wear combinations, existing failures, idle/staging/blips/mixed pedals, wheel/flywheel extremes and AWD modes.

Stock timing/rating data remain internally calibrated fictional vehicles, not claims of measured manufacturer quarter miles or component lifetime. Unsafe modified builds may legitimately fail. Native Windows checks cover real Update/OnGUI/DSP/input/save plumbing separately. Human sound/driving review, Android/device/controller validation, final graphics and the full operating-cost economy remain their explicit later work. Nitrous delivery and random intermittent faults remain deferred.
