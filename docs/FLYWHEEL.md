# Flywheel and crankshaft rotation

The race, AI and engine bench use a calculated flywheel at 120 Hz. This implements the light-flywheel tradeoff in `objective.md`: faster revving and faster RPM decay, with less stored energy available for a manual launch. Values are modeled prototype calibrations, not manufacturer specifications.

Each vehicle has flywheel mass, outer radius and inner radius. The current geometry is a uniform annular disc: `I = 0.5 × mass × (outerRadius² + innerRadius²)`. The garage/bench tune multiplies mass from 0.25 to 2.5; zero in an older tune means stock. Engine/core inertia is separate (`engineInertiaKgM2`, zero uses 0.12 kg m²). All free-rev, clutch, launch and converter calculations use core inertia plus the calculated flywheel inertia. Existing explicit engine-inertia values now describe the core, with the flywheel added separately; saved cash, purchases and records retain their existing format.

Stock modeled flywheels are 8 kg / 0.15 m for the hatch, 9 kg / 0.15 m for the coupe, 12 kg / 0.17 m for the V8 and 14 kg / 0.17 m for the pickup, with 0.025 m inner radii. Preparation authors these once and preserves subsequent tuning.

Neutral RPM changes according to net engine torque divided by total inertia. On release, drag `(18 + RPM × 0.003) Nm` consumes rotational energy until idle control holds the engine at idle. A heavier flywheel therefore takes longer to drop from the same starting RPM under the same drag; a lighter flywheel also spins up faster. Carburetor behavior, limiter cuts and load still affect the net torque. The test bench protects idle unless deliberate failure testing is enabled.

Changing gear does not instantly change RPM or vehicle speed. A manual shift disconnects shaft torque while the engine continues rotating and burning fuel according to the held pedal and limiter. Reconnection uses finite clutch capacity and reflected vehicle/wheel inertia. DCT handover retains part of clutch capacity. Converter lock-up also synchronizes through finite torque, releases during shifts and uses speed hysteresis; fluid load depends on pump/turbine speed difference. Driver rev-match assistance applies bounded synchronization instead of assigning RPM.

Engine horsepower represents combustion output, not stored energy. It may be zero on a throttle lift or limiter cut while the crankshaft still turns. Shaft torque can be zero with the clutch disconnected. The dyno's lower RPM trace shows rotation independently of its HP/Nm traces. Stored flywheel energy is `0.5 × I × angularSpeed²`; angular momentum is `I × angularSpeed`. Both, along with flywheel/total inertia, appear in bench diagnostics and race/bench CSV exports. Stored flywheel energy is not counted as additional combustion horsepower.

## Testing

The standard four-gearbox Windows fixture sizes a temporary clutch for its boosted engine plus enough torque headroom to synchronize the first-to-second flywheel speed difference over a nominal 0.20 seconds. Factory assets and saved stock clutch ratings stay unchanged. The bench **Scenarios** tab offers the same explicit sizing button; this is a development fixture adjustment, not a purchased upgrade. Additional gearbox synchronization/re-engagement time and changing road speed still affect the actual shift duration.

`shift-stock-clutch.csv` and `shift-matched-clutch.csv` compare uninterrupted full-gas shifts with identical ratios, boost, engine and road speed. The hatch's 3.45→2.05 ratio implies about 4160 RPM from 7000 at unchanged road speed after coupling. A slipping clutch can keep engine RPM much higher. The tested stock rating with gearbox stage 1 is 237.5 Nm versus 599.2 Nm for the fixture including synchronization headroom. Starting at 6900 RPM, the matched setup reaches a minimum of 4483 RPM and about 9 RPM slip within 0.75 s; the stock setup reaches 5361 RPM and still slips by 597 RPM. These are authored model values, not production clutch recommendations.

In the Engine test scene, choose **Use sliders → Engine** and edit the flywheel mass/radii or the tune's `flywheelMass` multiplier. The calculated inertias are displayed next to those settings. Keyboard takeover remains read-only until **Use sliders** is selected again. Garage tuning also exposes the flywheel mass multiplier; these development adjustments currently have no separate purchase price.

Capture A and B with different flywheels. **FlywheelCoastDown** holds 5000 RPM for two seconds with no gas, then releases the fixture for ten seconds of neutral decay. This gives both variants an identical starting speed. **DynamicPullAndShifts** uses actual inertia/load, shifts at five and seven seconds, and releases gas at nine seconds. **LoadedPullAndShifts** remains the fixed-RPM acoustic fixture: its imposed RPM sweep intentionally overrides physical coast/synchronization. Manual W/Space testing and neutral blips use the physical model when fixed RPM is off.

This is a reduced-order model. The bench has an assigned brake load and a ratio-derived virtual handover target, rather than a separately simulated dynamometer roller inertia. Clutch heat, torsional vibration, detailed flywheel mass distribution, gearbox/input-shaft inertia and persistent component wear remain future refinements. Engine-off in the bench remains an explicit stop/mute command; it does not simulate ignition-off coasting.
